Where the practice ranks

Two keyword sets, doing two different jobs.

Not every ranking is meant to fill a chair. The first set below is the commercial core — the terms a prospective patient types when they are ready to book. The second set is informational, and its job is authority: it earns the topical credibility that helps the commercial pages rank at all. Both matter. Confusing them is how SEO reporting starts to mislead.

How the ground was moved

Four disciplines, applied consistently for twelve months.

There was no single big move. The gains above are the compound result of a monthly cadence — technical hygiene, content built around real queries, and careful attention to keyword cannibalisation.

Cannibalisation-aware content strategy

Blog articles were commissioned only after auditing existing service pages for overlapping intent. New posts targeted informational queries — cavity healing, tongue injuries, filling repairs — that the service pages were never built to rank for. Top 3 positions rose from 49 to 134 without the service pages losing ground to their own blog.

Technical SEO groundwork

Ongoing attention to schema markup, meta title and description quality, internal linking to live URLs only, and monthly Semrush and Screaming Frog audits. Average position moved from 19.5 to 11.0 — an 8.5-position lift blended across the entire query portfolio.

Local search focus

The Saskatoon-modifier set — oral surgery, emergency dentist, pediatric dentistry, children's dentist — was prioritised above generic national terms. All four now sit inside the Top 8, and together they represent the highest-intent traffic the practice receives.

Authority built to support the commercial pages

Informational content and 488 referring domains were built for one purpose: making the treatment pages credible enough to rank. The bitten-tongue guide drew 302 visits in June, and that topical depth is what lets a single-location practice compete for terms like teeth cleaning at national scale.
